# Phase 2: Business Understanding

**Status: Complete**

## Business Context

Sales volume alone does not show which products create the most commercial value. A retailer can sell many low-priced units while revenue remains concentrated in a smaller set of premium products.

## Main Business Question

> Which products and categories drive electronics revenue, and how should decision-makers balance unit demand with revenue contribution?

## Business Problem

Merchandising and sales leaders need a consistent view of revenue, order activity, and units sold. Comparing those measures helps distinguish high-volume traffic drivers from high-value revenue drivers and shows how dependent the catalog is on a few products.

## Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Decision supported |
|---|---|
| Sales leadership | Monitor revenue and order performance |
| Merchandising or category managers | Compare product and category contribution |
| Marketing team | Identify products that attract volume or generate revenue |
| Executive leadership | Evaluate revenue concentration and reporting priorities |

## Business Objectives

1. Reconcile transaction data into reliable revenue, order, and unit KPIs.
2. Rank products and categories by revenue and volume.
3. Identify differences between unit demand and revenue contribution.
4. Measure how concentrated revenue is among leading products.
5. Provide an interactive Excel dashboard for market and category review.

## Success Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Decision supported |
|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | Sum of quantity multiplied by unit price | Measure gross sales value |
| Unique orders | Distinct order IDs | Measure order activity without line duplication |
| Units sold | Sum of quantity ordered | Measure product volume |
| Revenue share | Product or category revenue divided by total revenue | Measure concentration |

## Business Questions

1. Which products and categories generate the most revenue?
2. Which categories sell the most units?
3. Do volume leaders also lead revenue?
4. How much revenue is concentrated among the top products?
5. How do results change by city or product category?

## Assumptions and Limitations

- Each retained row represents a completed product line within an order.
- Revenue is gross sales revenue before costs, returns, taxes, and adjustments.
- The records cover portions of four months and do not support continuous seasonality analysis.
- Cost, margin, returns, customer, and inventory data are unavailable.
- Revenue contribution does not establish profitability.

## Main Limitation

> The dashboard identifies revenue and volume leaders, but it cannot recommend the most profitable products without cost, margin, returns, and inventory data.
